# First Principles

Ten. If Atlas OS were destroyed and only this file survived, the system could
be rebuilt.

1. **Text outlives tools.** Anything worth keeping 20 years is written in
   plain text under version control. Tools render; they never own.

2. **Knowledge compounds only when it has exactly one home.** A fact stored
   twice diverges; a fact stored nowhere is re-purchased at full price.

3. **Judgment must be written to survive.** Unwritten taste dies with its
   holder and cannot be delegated — to people or to models.

4. **Outputs are compiled; sources are curated.** Work products derive from
   identity, knowledge, and taste. Improving a source improves every future
   output; polishing an output improves one.

5. **Abstraction is bought with the second use.** Generalize nothing on first
   use; question everything unused after promotion. Wrong abstractions cost
   more than duplication.

6. **Every loop closes with learning or it wasn't a loop.** Work that ends
   without changing the system was rented, not owned.

7. **The simplest thing that fully works, then stop.** Complexity is a loan
   against future attention, at compounding interest.

8. **Humans gate the irreversible.** Automation may draft, filter, and
   propose without limit; it may never publish, spend, price, or promise.

9. **Provenance or it didn't happen.** Every output records what produced it;
   every decision records why. Untraceable success can't be repeated;
   untraceable failure can't be fixed.

10. **The system serves the portfolio, never itself.** Time spent maintaining
    Atlas is overhead unless it demonstrably compounds. When in doubt: delete
    structure, keep knowledge.
