# Rejections

What we refuse is more defining than what we admire. This list is consulted
**before** work starts (brief, moodboard, direction, pitch) — a direction
matching a rejection is dead on arrival, no critique needed. Complements
`anti-patterns.md`, which catches defects in work already produced; this file
prevents the work from being conceived.

Each entry: the rejection, why it's permanent, and the boundary (what is NOT
covered, so the veto stays sharp instead of swallowing good work).

## Overused gradients

Gradient-as-decoration — mesh backgrounds, purple-teal washes, gradient
text — applied because the toolchain makes it easy. **Why:** it is the
fastest-dating visual signature of any era, and it signals template, not
intent. **Boundary:** gradients that model something real (light on a
surface, depth in data visualization) are craft, not decoration.

## Generic AI illustrations

Recognizable-model-output art: waxy 3D mascots, dream-smooth portraits,
incoherent detail on inspection. **Why:** it is the new stock photo —
instantly read as "no budget, no care", and it poisons the honesty principle
because the style itself announces automation. **Boundary:** AI-assisted
imagery that passes a professional art-direction pass (consistent light,
intentional composition, no artifacts) and fits the brand's documented
visual language is judged as imagery, not by its tool.

## Fake luxury branding

Luxury costume without luxury substance: thin serifs + gold foil + black
everything on a mid-market offer. **Why:** luxury is an operational promise
(materials, service, scarcity, time); wearing its clothes without keeping
its promises is the borrowed-authority anti-pattern at brand scale, and
customers detect it in one interaction. **Boundary:** genuine premium
positioning (MD's Lux) may use luxury codes — *after* the operational
promises exist and are documented in the business's DNA and offers.

## Copycat Apple aesthetics

"Make it like Apple": vast white space, floating product on gradient,
SF-adjacent type, cinematic product video — applied to businesses that are
not Apple. **Why:** it invites a comparison we lose, communicates nothing
about the actual business, and is the most saturated visual cliché in
commerce. **Boundary:** the underlying principles Apple *applies* (ruthless
reduction, hierarchy, craft) are Atlas principles too — extracted and
applied natively, never worn as a skin.

## Trendy but shallow design

Any direction whose main argument is "this is what everyone is doing now":
current-year illustration styles, layout fads, typography trends adopted
without a business reason. **Why:** trends are depreciation schedules — the
work is dated the moment the trend turns, and the 20-year horizon makes
this a guaranteed loss. **Boundary:** a trend may be adopted when it
*independently* satisfies the brand's documented principles — then it's not
trend-following, it's convergence, and it must be defensible without
mentioning popularity.

## Overcomplicated logos

Marks that need detail to exist: gradients inside logos, fine linework,
multiple concepts fused, anything that dies at 16px or in one color.
**Why:** a logo's entire job is recognition under degradation (small, fast,
mono, blurred); complexity is failure at the job itself — see the
photocopy/blur tests in QUALITY_BENCHMARKS. **Boundary:** rich emblems and
crests are legitimate *secondary* assets in identities that also possess a
simple primary mark.

## Weak typography

System-default type thinking: faux bold/italic, tight tracking on caps,
five sizes per screen, novelty display faces doing body work, text as
afterthought. **Why:** typography is 90% of most business communication;
weakness there is weakness everywhere — and it is the single cheapest place
to look expensive (QUALITY_BENCHMARKS: luxurious typography). **Boundary:**
deliberate typographic minimalism (one family, two sizes) is the opposite
of weak — weakness is carelessness, not restraint.

## Decorative animation with no purpose

Motion because the framework ships with it: scroll-jacking, parallax
everywhere, elements flying in, hover gymnastics. **Why:** motion taxes
attention — spent without informational or emotional return, it reads as
noise and slows task completion. **Boundary:** motion that explains
(state change, causality, spatial continuity) or that carries brand feeling
in ONE deliberate signature moment is craft. Test: describe what the
animation communicates in one sentence, or cut it.

## Empty marketing buzzwords

"Innovative solutions", "seamless experience", "next-generation",
"revolutionizing", "empowering" — vocabulary that predicates nothing.
**Why:** buzzwords are claims with the evidence removed; they occupy the
exact sentence position where a specific, provable statement should live
(specificity principle; anti-patterns: AI-flavored prose). **Boundary:**
technical terms of art with real referents are not buzzwords, even when
fashionable.

## Protocol

- Checked at direction stage by whoever briefs (human or agent) — patterns
  that compile briefs must include this file in stage 3 alongside principles.
- A rejection may be overridden only by the owner, in writing, in the brief,
  with a reason — overrides are logged in the retro (taste-evolution signal,
  same as principle overrides).
- New rejections enter through the learning loop: a direction that felt
  wrong and kept feeling wrong across retros gets an entry with its "why"
  and its boundary. Entries without a boundary are not accepted — a veto
  that can't state its edges bans good work.
