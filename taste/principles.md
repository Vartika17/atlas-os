# Principles

Labeled sections; the context chain pulls sections by name (`taste_sections:`
in patterns). Each section ≤150 tokens — taste that can't be stated tightly
isn't understood yet. Derivation and exemplars live in `taste/references/`;
these are the invariants that recurred across unrelated domains and decades.
v1.0.0 is research-derived (objective excellence first); the owner's
calibration lands as minor bumps when principles decide real critiques.

## systems

Design the system before the artifact. Written rules — grid, type logic,
color ratios, motion durations, voice vocabulary — outlast any single
execution and make the tenth piece cheaper and better than the first. Taste
living in one head is a liability: codify until a stranger (or the smallest
model) produces on-brand work from the written rules alone. That is the
test.

## consistency

Recognition compounds with uninterrupted years of identical application;
every change spends equity. Fix the invariants — assets, placements, codes,
names — and vary the surface freely: campaigns rotate, the skeleton never
does. Evolve inherited marks; never reset them. Flexing the system is a
privilege earned by prior rigidity. Test: does this change buy more than
the recognition it burns?

## restraint

Remove until removal hurts. Emptiness is purchased attention: one focal
point, one call to action, one motion event per moment. Every deleted
element raises the perceived confidence of what remains; leaving the wall
blank takes more authority than filling it. Decoration that carries no
meaning is a small lie. Test: remove the element — if meaning survives, it
was noise.

## hierarchy

One idea per unit — slide, section, screen, frame, sentence. The eye must
know what comes first, second, third. Hierarchy comes from contrast in
size, weight, and space, never from decoration: needing an ornament to
signal importance means the hierarchy already failed. Structure carried by
headings, grouping, and sequence survives every trend; structure carried by
color and imagery dies with the trend that styled it.

## specificity

Concrete beats abstract, always. One researched, verifiable detail
outperforms any stack of adjectives, and the reader's self-drawn conclusion
beats your stated one. Claims travel with their proof in the same breath.
Named humans with numbers beat logo walls and star ratings. Test: strike
every adjective — does the argument still stand?

## honesty

Every claim needs an operational receipt: scarcity only when real, urgency
only when true, luxury codes only after luxury promises are kept, a story
only if operations can cash it. Admitting a priced-in weakness buys belief
for every strength. Honest work ages into patina; implied lies age into
decay — and a discovered lie destroys more value than it ever added.

## protagonist

The customer is the hero; the business is the mentor or the tool. Enter
through an emotion the audience already has, staged as a concrete scene in
their own words. Respect their intelligence: work the audience completes —
a conclusion drawn, a connection made — is remembered as their achievement
and credited to the brand. Any draft in which the company is the hero gets
rewritten before it ships.

## degradation

Design for the worst condition the work will actually meet: small, fast,
monochrome, tired reader, slow connection, cheap screen, secondhand
retelling. Excellence in the hard case guarantees the easy ones; a design
that only works in the portfolio mock is a defect. Tests: photocopy and
blur for marks; worst-device for pages; titles-only skim for documents and
decks.

## durability

The ten-year test: an idea that must be replaced next season was an
execution, never a strategy. Trends are depreciation schedules. Verbal and
visual assets are 30-year infrastructure — write taglines and choose
typefaces accordingly. Prefer the choice that ages into a signature over
the one that peaks at launch. Test: would we still fund this in year ten?

## friction

The audience's time is a design material — spend it like their money.
Never ask for what was already given; never force relearning of what was
habit; deliver first value before first demand. Speed is a revenue
decision, on every device, in every decade. Measure task completion, not
engagement: if they finish faster and leave sooner, it worked.

## craft

Details are the message: even type texture, aligned edges, consistent
naming, designed empty and error states, rehearsed logic. The audience
cannot name these but feels their absence as cheapness — and presumes the
invisible work equally careless. Production gloss cannot rescue missing
logic; the argument is the deliverable. Budget the last 5%; it is where
trust is manufactured.
