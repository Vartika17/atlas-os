# Principles

Labeled sections; the context chain pulls sections by name
(`taste_sections:` in patterns). Keep each section under ~150 tokens — taste
that can't be stated tightly isn't understood yet.

Seeded with defensible defaults. Each section graduates from draft only after
it has decided at least one real critique.

## restraint

Good work removes until removal hurts. One idea per piece, one call to action,
one focal point. If everything is emphasized, nothing is. Decoration that
doesn't carry meaning is debt.

## hierarchy

The eye must know where to go first, second, third — in layout, in copy, in
code. If a reader must work out the order themselves, the piece has failed at
its only structural job.

## specificity

Concrete beats abstract, always. A number beats an adjective; an example beats
a claim; a named customer scenario beats "businesses like yours". Vagueness is
what no-taste looks like in text.

## honesty

Nothing ships that overpromises. The work should look and read like what the
business actually delivers on a normal day, not its best day. Trust compounds
across 20 years; cleverness doesn't.

## craft

Details are the message. Alignment, spacing, consistent terminology, clean
typography — the reader can't name them but feels their absence as
"cheapness". Budget time for the last 5%.
