# Websites — Reference Study

A website wins commercially when strangers can complete their job faster there than anywhere else, repeatedly, for years.
Excellence here is measured in habit formation and zero-relearning cost across decades of visits — not in first-impression polish.
The exemplars below survived every redesign trend by refusing most of them.

## Wikipedia (2001–present)

- **Why world-class:** A top-10 global website for two decades with near-zero marketing spend. Every article uses one identical
  template — title, lead summary, infobox, contents, references — so a first-time visitor already knows how to read their
  millionth page. Load-fast text-first pages made it the default answer surface for search engines, which compounded traffic.
- **Design principles:** Radical template consistency; content-is-the-interface; progressive depth
  (lead paragraph → sections → citations).
- **Business problem solved:** Earning enough trust that volunteers donate labor and readers donate money — with no ads to fund
  credibility-destroying incentives.
- **Psychology:** Authority via visible sourcing (citations as trust signals); consistency reduces cognitive load to near zero;
  the donation appeal works through reciprocity after years of free value.
- **UX principles:** Every page is an entry page (SEO reality — no assumed journey); dense internal linking rewards curiosity;
  edit history and talk pages make error tolerance and correction visible.
- **Branding principles:** The plain look IS the brand — visual austerity signals neutrality. A redesign toward "modern" styling
  would read as editorial slant.
- **Visual principles:** One column of readable text, near-default system type, blue links untouched since the 90s, whitespace
  only where structure needs it. Ugly-on-purpose, legible always.
- **Learn:** When users visit thousands of your pages, template consistency beats per-page art direction by orders of magnitude.
- **Do NOT copy:** The austerity. A commercial brand needs distinctiveness; Wikipedia can look generic because its content
  monopoly is the moat.
- **Suitable industries:** Documentation, knowledge bases, publishers, directories, any catalog with >1k pages.

## Craigslist (1995–present)

- **Why world-class:** Nearly unchanged since the 90s yet reportedly served billions of monthly pageviews and gutted newspaper
  classifieds globally. A single page of plain links exposes the entire inventory taxonomy at once — no hero, no imagery,
  no onboarding. Posting an ad takes minutes with no account friction in most categories.
- **Design principles:** Full-catalog visibility on one screen; speed as the feature; never break returning users' muscle memory.
- **Business problem solved:** Liquidity. Classifieds live or die on both sides showing up; every ounce of friction removed
  compounds listings, which compounds buyers.
- **Psychology:** Familiarity breeds trust in transactions between strangers; the unpolished look signals "no corporate
  middleman," which fits the peer-to-peer promise; zero dark patterns sustains 30 years of goodwill.
- **UX principles:** Link density over progressive disclosure — scanning beats clicking when users know what they want; instant
  page loads on any connection; forgiving, minimal forms.
- **Branding principles:** Anti-design as identity. Craigslist is instantly recognizable precisely because it ignored every
  trend; consistency over 30 years is the trust asset.
- **Visual principles:** Default-blue links, tiny type, no images on index pages, structure carried entirely by column grouping
  and headings.
- **Learn:** For utility products with habitual users, interface stability is a retention feature. Count the cost of every
  redesign in relearning time.
- **Do NOT copy:** The neglect. Craigslist could ignore mobile-era UX and safety tooling only because network effects locked in;
  a challenger with this UI would look untrustworthy, and marketplace verticals successfully unbundled its weak categories.
- **Suitable industries:** Marketplaces, classifieds, job boards, community utilities with two-sided liquidity.

## GOV.UK (2012–present)

- **Why world-class:** Consolidated roughly 1,800 separate UK government sites into one, won Design of the Year 2013, and became
  the reference model copied by governments worldwide. Task completion (renew a passport, pay tax) drives every decision;
  content is rewritten to a reading age most citizens can manage; every pattern is user-tested and published openly.
- **Design principles:** "Start with user needs"; one thing per page; do the hard work to make it simple; design in the open.
- **Business problem solved:** Cutting the cost of failure demand — every citizen who completes a task online is a phone call,
  letter, or office visit the state doesn't pay for.
- **Psychology:** One-question-per-page eliminates form abandonment from perceived effort; plain language removes status anxiety
  around bureaucracy; consistent patterns across all departments transfer trust from any service to every service.
- **UX principles:** Linear task flows with explicit start pages; accessibility (WCAG) as a hard requirement, not a nice-to-have;
  error messages written to say exactly what to do next.
- **Branding principles:** A single crown, one typeface (GDS Transport), black-and-white restraint — authority expressed through
  clarity rather than ornament. The absence of departmental logos is itself the message: one government.
- **Visual principles:** Large type, generous line-height, narrow measure for reading, yellow focus states, links and buttons
  visually unmistakable. Nothing decorative exists.
- **Learn:** Define every page by the task it completes, then delete everything that doesn't serve that task. Measure completion
  rate, not engagement.
- **Do NOT copy:** The total suppression of persuasion. GOV.UK has a captive audience with no competitor; commercial sites still
  must sell, differentiate, and create desire.
- **Suitable industries:** Government, banking, insurance, healthcare, utilities, legal — anywhere users arrive anxious and must
  finish a task.

## Timeless principles extracted

- Optimize for the returning visitor: template consistency and interface stability compound into habit, and habit is retention.
  Any redesign must justify its relearning cost.
- Speed is a design decision with revenue attached. A page that loads and reads instantly beats a beautiful one that makes users
  wait, on every device, in every decade.
- Every page is a landing page. Design each URL to orient a cold visitor (what is this, where am I, what next) because search —
  not your homepage — is the front door.
- Match visual register to the promise: austerity signals neutrality (Wikipedia), plainness signals peer-to-peer (Craigslist),
  restraint signals authority (GOV.UK). Styling that contradicts the promise erodes trust regardless of its craft.
- Measure task completion, not engagement. If users finish faster and leave sooner, the site is working.
- Structure over decoration: hierarchy carried by headings, grouping, and link text survives every visual trend; hierarchy
  carried by color and imagery dies with the trend.
